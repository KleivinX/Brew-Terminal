## The short version

The **code** is free software under AGPL-3.0-or-later. Fork it, modify it, redistribute it.

The **name, logo and visual identity** are not. They identify this project and who stands
behind it, and that only means something if it stays accurate.

## What is covered

- The name "Brew Terminal"
- The Brew Terminal logo, wordmark and icon artwork
- The distinctive combination of brand colours and typography as used in official materials
- Any claim, direct or implied, that something is official, endorsed, or affiliated

## Forks

You may fork the code. If you distribute your fork, you must:

1. **Give it a different name.** Not "Brew Terminal", and not a name likely to be confused with it. "Brew Terminal Plus", "BrewTerminal 2" and "Brew Terminal Community Edition" are all confusing; pick something that is yours.
2. **Remove the Brew Terminal logo and artwork**, and replace them with your own.
3. **Not imply endorsement.** You may factually state "based on Brew Terminal" or "a fork of Brew Terminal", with a link to the original. You may not say "official", "certified", "approved", "partnered", or anything a reasonable person would read as approval by this project.
4. **Keep the AGPL obligations** that come with the code itself. Those are separate from this policy.

## What you may always do without asking

- Use the name to refer to this project accurately — in articles, reviews, comparisons, talks, tutorials, forum posts and packaging metadata.
- State that your fork is derived from Brew Terminal.
- Reproduce the logo unmodified when writing _about_ the project, in a way that does not suggest affiliation.

## What needs permission

- Using the name or logo in the name, icon or branding of your own product or service
- Merchandise
- Domain names, social accounts or app-store listings using the name
- Anything presenting itself as an official distribution

## Reporting misuse

Contact the project owner through the repository's issue tracker or the contact address in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Why this exists

AGPL protects users' freedom to run, study, modify and share the software. It does not stop
someone shipping a modified build under the same name — including one that adds trade
execution, sells user data, or gives investment advice, all of which this project deliberately
refuses to do. Keeping the name separate is how a user can tell the difference.
